Spelling consistency and tone tips for Roman Hindi messages

  • Roman Hindi does not have one universal spelling standard. Pick spellings your recipient recognises and keep them consistent within the same message.
  • Choose “aap” or “tum” according to the relationship and keep the pronouns consistent; switching register mid-message can sound awkward.
  • Use English words when they are part of how you normally speak, not simply to decorate a Hindi sentence. If the recipient prefers Devanagari, choose the Hindi collection instead.

ROMAN HINDI STYLE GUIDE

Keeping Hinglish readable across different phones and generations

Hinglish (Hindi written in the Latin alphabet blended with conversational English) is the natural language of millions of daily chats across India. However, because Roman Hindi has no single dictionary spelling, overly long sentences or unusual phonetic spellings can slow the reader down. Keep Hinglish messages punchy, use familiar spellings (such as “khushiyan”, “apnapan”, “dua”, “parivaar”), and avoid mixing three different pronoun registers in one line.

Best occasions for Hinglish

Sibling Rakhi notes, friend birthdays, Holi/Diwali WhatsApp groups, and 9:16 Instagram or WhatsApp Status cards.

When to switch to pure Hindi or English

For formal wedding invitations, official client greetings, or older relatives who prefer reading Devanagari script.
