How to write natural, idiomatic English wishes for Indian and global occasions

A useful English greeting is clear, sincere and suited to the relationship. Use a concise line for a status, a warmer paragraph for a card or a respectful note for a colleague. Do not make a message sound more formal than you would normally speak.

Practical rules for clear, sincere English card writing

  • Use familiar, plain English when the recipient may be reading in a second language; avoid idioms that could make a sincere wish confusing.
  • For a workplace message, use a clear greeting and a simple sign-off. Keep the tone positive without making personal assumptions or promises.
  • For family and friends, replace a string of adjectives with one concrete quality, memory or shared activity.

Choosing between warm conversational English and formal business English

English greetings in India often bridge multiple generations and workplace cultures. A message that sounds natural in a college friend group may feel too casual for a senior colleague or family elder. The most effective English wishes rely on concrete nouns and active verbs rather than chains of ornate adjectives.

Conversational English (Friends & Peers)

Use direct openings like “So glad to celebrate you today” or “Here’s to a calmer, happier year ahead” paired with one specific shared memory.

Formal English (Clients, Mentors & Elders)

Use complete sentences such as “Wishing you and your family good health and continued peace this festive season” and sign off with your full name.
